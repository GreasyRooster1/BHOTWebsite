The Film

A mind that
bent the cosmos

From the mind that redefined our understanding of the universe comes an extraordinary cinematic vision. This adaptation of Stephen Hawking's legendary work brings cosmology to breathtaking, intimate life.

Weaving personal biography with visual explorations of black holes, the Big Bang, and the nature of time itself, the film asks the questions Hawking spent his life pursuing: where did we come from, and does time have a beginning?

Shot across Cambridge, CERN, and the Atacama Desert, with astrophysical visualizations developed in collaboration with Caltech and the ESA.

Hawking's Life

1942Born in Oxford, England, on the 300th anniversary of Galileo's death.
1963Diagnosed with motor neurone disease at 21; given two years to live.
1970Proves that black holes must have a singularity with Roger Penrose.
1974Proposes Hawking radiation — black holes slowly evaporate over time.
1988A Brief History of Time published; sells 10 million copies in a decade.
2018Dies at 76 in Cambridge — having outlived his prognosis by 55 years.

Black Holes

Regions of spacetime where gravity is so extreme that nothing — not even light — can escape. At their centre lies a singularity, where density becomes infinite and known physics breaks down.

The Big Bang

13.8 billion years ago, the universe erupted from an infinitely hot, dense point. Space, time, and matter all began in this single moment — there was no "before."

t = 0, T → ∞

Arrow of Time

Why does time only flow forward? Hawking links the thermodynamic arrow of time — entropy always increases — to the cosmological expansion of the universe itself.

ΔS ≥ 0
⊛

Quantum Gravity

The great unification problem: reconciling Einstein's general relativity with quantum mechanics into a single "theory of everything." Hawking's no-boundary proposal imagines a universe with no edge in time.
